\title{\textbf{Reorganizing cuADMM} --- revision 2\\[4pt]
\large An ADMM-first, cone-extensible architecture with first-class free variables,\\
memory-aware PSD-projection strategies, and direct (and later preconditioned) linear solvers}
\author{Reorganization plan for the cuADMM developers}
\date{October 20, 2026\\[2pt]
\small Baseline: \code{main} at \code{2d8a755} (submodule \code{psd_projection} at \code{b566590}).\\
\small Revision 2 answers the review of October 20, 2026 \cite{review20} and the $\sigma$-rule study of October 19 \cite{sigmastudy}.}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
cuADMM is a GPU solver for large multi-block conic programs, built around the first-order method of
\cite{cuadmm} and the factorization-free PSD projection of \cite{composite}. Its numerics are sound and
well tested, but nearly all logic lives in one class (\code{SDPSolver}) whose \code{solve()} method spans
about 1{,}080 lines; configuration is spread over positional arguments, public fields and preprocessor
constants; and several algorithmic options are compiled out or unreachable. This plan reorganizes the code
into a layered library in which (i) plain ADMM with step length $\tau\in(0,2)$ is the default and sGS-ADMM
is a runtime option, justified by the equivalence result of \cite{afterimage}; (ii) the penalty parameter
follows a residual-balancing rule that \emph{stops} changing (a freeze), so that the fixed-$\sigma$ theory
applies; (iii) every cone is a plug-in, with free variables treated as a first-class cone; (iv) the PSD
projection is a catalog of named strategies whose memory model differs by block size---batched and
throughput-oriented for small and medium blocks, one block at a time through a shared arena for large
blocks; and (v) the normal-equation solve is an interface that supports sparse and dense direct methods now
and leaves room for inexact, preconditioned iterative solves. The plan lists the defects found so far, the
target architecture, a single table of options, a test strategy, and an incremental migration (a pre-step
and phases 0--10) with acceptance criteria.
\end{abstract}

\section{Summary}\label{sec:summary}

\paragraph{Decisions.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{D\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{ADMM is the default algorithm.} Plain two-block ADMM with multiplier step length
  $\tau = 1.618$; $\tau = 1.918$ stays available as an aggressive setting, and any $\tau\in(0,2)$ is
  accepted. sGS-ADMM becomes a \emph{runtime} option (today it is removed at compile time by
  \code{CUADMM_PLAIN_ADMM_ONLY} in the CMake library). Both methods iterate the same fixed-point map in the
  $z$-variable \cite{afterimage} (Section~\ref{sec:math-admm}).
  \item \textbf{The penalty rule must stop changing $\sigma$.} The default policy balances the residuals
  early and then freezes $\sigma$, so that its total variation is finite and the fixed-$\sigma$ convergence
  theory applies (Section~\ref{sec:penalty}). The historical rule is restored under its historical name
  \code{legacy_adaptive}, and the October 13 rule now on \code{main} becomes \code{residual_balancing}.
  \item \textbf{Linear systems: direct methods now, iterative methods later.} Sparse direct (cuDSS on the
  GPU, CHOLMOD on the CPU) and dense direct (cuSOLVER) backends sit behind one interface that already
  carries the hooks for inexact, preconditioned iterative solves (Section~\ref{sec:linsys}). cuDSS is the
  intended default once the benchmark of Section~\ref{sec:bench} confirms it.
  \item \textbf{Composite polynomial filtering is integrated directly}, in FP16 only, as the default warm-up
  of large blocks with $n\ge5000$; the \code{psd_projection} submodule is retired after parity tests pass
  (Section~\ref{sec:composite}).
  \item \textbf{Cones are plug-ins, and free variables are first class.} Free, nonnegative and PSD blocks
  are ported to one \code{Cone} interface; every component states what it does for free variables
  (Section~\ref{sec:free}).
  \item \textbf{Memory-aware PSD projection.} Small and medium blocks are processed in batches for
  throughput; large blocks are processed one at a time through one shared arena sized for the largest
  block, which cuts the large-tier memory from about $7\sum_b n_b^2$ to about $5\,n_{\max}^2$ doubles
  (Section~\ref{sec:psd}).
  \item \textbf{Software-engineering standards.} One options object with one table of defaults, explicit
  ownership of GPU resources, no allocation in the iteration loop, deterministic by default, consistent
  naming, option and policy names that never silently change meaning, and a migration in which every
  structural step reproduces the current iterates bit for bit.
  \item \textbf{Data formats.} A native HDF5 format for very large problems, and readers for SDPA, SeDuMi,
  SDPT3 and MOSEK MATLAB data and the legacy text files, all producing one \code{ProblemData}
  (Section~\ref{sec:io}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What changed since revision 1.} Every recommendation of the October 20 review \cite{review20}
is adopted or answered; Appendix~\ref{app:review} maps each one to the section that addresses it. The
main changes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a new penalty plan (balance, then freeze) with a separate decision step, Phase~4b;
  \item a pre-step (``Pre-0'') that restores \code{legacy_adaptive}, names the edited rule, and records the
  policy in the summary;
  \item a rewritten PSD section with an explicit, measured memory model for large blocks;
  \item the composite filter reduced to FP16, on by default for large blocks with $n\ge5000$;
  \item a new section on input and output formats, including MOSEK's MATLAB format used by SPOT;
  \item a new section on free variables;
  \item interface hooks and a plan for preconditioned iterative linear solves;
  \item a smaller first milestone, Phases 8--9 deferred, and corrected facts and citations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and the three papers}\label{sec:background}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{cuADMM} \cite{cuadmm} introduced the solver: sGS-ADMM for multi-block SDPs, PSD
  projection by cuSOLVER (batched Jacobi for many small blocks, QR-based EVD on streams for larger
  ones), and a hybrid CPU--GPU normal-equation solve with CHOLMOD, building on the trajectory
  optimization pipeline of \cite{wafr}. Its experiments target sparse moment relaxations of
  contact-rich planning and multi-block SDPs from Mittelmann's collection.
  \item \textbf{Composite polynomial filtering} \cite{composite} replaces the eigenvalue decomposition
  of a large dense block by a short composition of odd degree-5 polynomials evaluated with GEMMs in
  FP16 or FP32, after scaling the spectrum with a Lanczos estimate of the norm. It is designed as a
  low-precision \emph{warm start} inside ADMM, followed by FP64 projections, and notes that ADMM quickly
  enters a low-rank regime \cite{kang2025local} in which LOBPCG becomes attractive.
  \item \textbf{Afterimage slow regions} \cite{afterimage} studies first-order methods for linear
  conic programs as fixed-point iterations. Its Table~1 and Appendix~A.2 show that ADMM and sGS-ADMM
  induce the \emph{same} fixed-point map in the auxiliary variable $z$, which is the basis of
  decision D1.
\end{itemize}
Two internal documents shaped this revision: the $\sigma$-rule study of October 19 \cite{sigmastudy},
which replicated the solver on the CPU decision for decision against 13 GPU runs, and the review of
revision 1 of October 20 \cite{review20}. Numbers quoted from them are labelled as such.

\section{Mathematical specification}\label{sec:math}

This section fixes the mathematics the code must implement; class and function names in later sections
refer to the symbols introduced here (Appendix~\ref{app:notation}).

\subsection{Problem class}\label{sec:problem}
Let $\mathbb{X} = \mathbb{X}_1\times\cdots\times\mathbb{X}_B$ and
$\K = \K_1\times\cdots\times\K_B$ with each $\K_\beta$ a closed convex cone in $\mathbb{X}_\beta$.
cuADMM solves the primal--dual pair
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pd}
\min_{X\in\mathbb{X}}\ \inner{C}{X}\ \ \text{s.t.}\ \ \A X = b,\ X\in\K,
\qquad
\max_{y\in\R^m,\ S\in\mathbb{X}}\ \inner{b}{y}\ \ \text{s.t.}\ \ \Ad y + S = C,\ S\in\Kd .
\end{equation}
Today $\K_\beta\in\{\Sym^n_+,\ \R^n_+,\ \R^n\}$ (block codes \code{s}, \code{l}, \code{u}), with dual
cones $\Sym^n_+$, $\R^n_+$ and $\{0\}$. A \emph{free} block ($\K_\beta = \R^n$) leaves $X_\beta$ unrestricted
and turns the dual constraint on its rows into the equality $\A_\beta^* y = C_\beta$
(Section~\ref{sec:free}). PSD blocks are stored in $\svec$ form (upper triangle, column by column,
off-diagonal entries multiplied by $\sqrt2$), so $\inner{\cdot}{\cdot}$ is the Euclidean inner product on
the stacked vector.

\subsection{ADMM, sGS-ADMM and their equivalence}\label{sec:math-admm}

\paragraph{ADMM (default).} With penalty $\sigma>0$ and step length $\tau\in(0,2)$, one iteration is
\begin{align}
  y^{k+1}   &= (\A\Ad)^{-1}\big[\sigma^{-1}b - \A(\sigma^{-1}X^k + S^k - C)\big], && \text{(y-step)}\label{eq:ystep}\\
  S^{k+1}   &= \Proj_{\Kd}\big(C - \Ad y^{k+1} - \sigma^{-1}X^k\big), && \text{(cone step)}\label{eq:sstep}\\
  X^{k+1}   &= X^k + \tau\sigma\big(\Ad y^{k+1} + S^{k+1} - C\big). && \text{(multiplier step)}\label{eq:xstep}
\end{align}
This is the classical three-step ADMM for SDP \cite{wen}, exactly (12a)--(12c) of \cite{afterimage} and
(24a)--(24c) of \cite{composite} with $\tau$, and what \code{SDPSolver::solve} executes when
\code{switch_admm = 0}.

\paragraph{sGS-ADMM (option).} sGS-ADMM \cite{chen2017,cuadmm} inserts a second y-step after the cone
step: $y^{k+1/2}$ from \eqref{eq:ystep}, $S^{k+1}$ from \eqref{eq:sstep} with $y^{k+1/2}$, then
$y^{k+1} = (\A\Ad)^{-1}[\sigma^{-1}b - \A(\sigma^{-1}X^k + S^{k+1} - C)]$, then \eqref{eq:xstep}. It costs one
extra normal-equation solve per iteration.

\paragraph{Equivalence \cite{afterimage}.} Let $P_\A = \A^{\dagger}\A$ with
$\A^{\dagger} = \Ad(\A\Ad)^{-1}$, $P_\A^{\perp} = I - P_\A$, and define
$z^k := P_\A^{\perp}X^k + \A^{\dagger}b - \sigma P_\A S^k - \sigma P_\A^{\perp}C$. Under $\A\Ad\succ0$,
$\sigma>0$ and $\tau\in(0,2)$, both methods generate $z^{k+1} = T(z^k)$ with the same map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zmap}
T(z) = z - P_\A\big[\Proj_{\K}(z) - \A^{\dagger}b\big] - \tau P_\A^{\perp}\big[-\Proj_{\Kd}(-z) + \sigma C\big]
\end{equation}
(\cite[Table~1, eqs.~(13) and (21)]{afterimage}). They differ only in how $(X,y,S)$ is recovered from $z$
(\cite[eqs.~(14) and (22)]{afterimage}), hence in the KKT residuals one observes. At fixed $\sigma$ the map
$T$ is $\alpha$-averaged with $\alpha = \max\{1,\tau\}/2$ in the metric $G = P_\A + \tau^{-1}P_\A^{\perp}$,
which is why every $\tau\in(0,2)$ is admissible \cite{afterimage}; convergence of the full sequence is
shown in \cite{chen2021}. The review found the equivalence to hold numerically to about $10^{-13}$
\cite{review20}.

\paragraph{Step length and the residual vote.} The multiplier step gives
$\A X^{k+1} - b = (1-\tau)(\A X^k - b) + \tau s^{k+1}$, where $s^{k+1} = \sigma\A(S^{k+1}-S^k)$ is the ADMM
dual residual \cite{sigmastudy}. The primal residual that the penalty rule watches is therefore a
filtered signal whose gain on sign-alternating components is $\tau/(2-\tau)$: 4.24 at $\tau = 1.618$ and
23.4 at $\tau = 1.918$. A larger $\tau$ makes the residual vote of Section~\ref{sec:penalty} noisier, so
$\tau$ is chosen first at fixed $\sigma$ and the penalty rule is re-screened at the chosen $\tau$ (Q4 in
Section~\ref{sec:decisions}).

\begin{decision}{Consequences for the design}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item One iteration engine with an \code{IterationScheme} strategy (\code{Admm}, \code{SgsAdmm}); no
  compile-time switch.
  \item $\tau$ is a validated option in $(0,2)$, default 1.618. The hidden $\tau$ rules of today's sGS branch
  ($\tau=1.95$, reduced to $\max(1.618,\tau/1.1)$ when $\eta_d<\text{tol}$) are removed.
  \item The derivations of (13) and (21) in \cite{afterimage} use only the definition of $z^k$, so a mid-run
  switch between the two schemes at fixed $\sigma$ continues the same $z$-orbit: the hybrid mode is a pure
  recovery choice. Whether to keep it is decided after a plain-ADMM run with $\tau=1.95$ (Q5).
  \item No penalty rule reads sGS residuals: the sGS primal residual contracts by exactly $|1-\tau|$ per
  iteration whatever the progress, and in the September campaign $\sigma$ climbed to $800$--$10^6$ on every
  stalled problem \cite{review20}.
\end{itemize}
\end{decision}

\subsection{Cone step for a product cone}
The cone step \eqref{eq:sstep} decomposes block by block,
$\Proj_{\Kd}(Z) = (\Proj_{\Kd_1}(Z_1),\dots,\Proj_{\Kd_B}(Z_B))$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item PSD: $\Proj_{\Sym^n_+}(Z) = Q\max(\Lambda,0)Q^{\top}$ for $Z = Q\Lambda Q^{\top}$ (self-dual);
  \item nonnegative orthant: $\max(Z,0)$ entrywise (self-dual);
  \item free block: $\Kd = \{0\}$, so $S_\beta = 0$.
\end{itemize}
For cones that are not self-dual, Moreau's decomposition $Z = \Proj_{\K}(Z) - \Proj_{\Kd}(-Z)$ gives
$\Proj_{\Kd}(Z) = Z + \Proj_{\K}(-Z)$, so a cone may implement whichever projection is cheaper. The same
identity gives the PSD projection from the negative side, $\Proj_{\Sym^n_+}(Z) = Z + \Proj_{\Sym^n_+}(-Z)$,
which the large-block strategies use when the negative part has lower rank (Section~\ref{sec:large-alg}).

\subsection{Scaling and KKT residuals}\label{sec:math-scaling}
The solver works on a scaled problem (on by default; Section~\ref{sec:scaling}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item rows of $\A$ and entries of $b$ are divided by $\nu_i = \max(1,\norm{A_i}_2)$, and $y_i$ is multiplied by $\nu_i$;
  \item with $b_s = 1+\norm{\tilde b}_2$ and $C_s = 1+\norm{C}_2$: $b\leftarrow b/b_s$, $X\leftarrow X/b_s$,
  $C\leftarrow C/C_s$, $S\leftarrow S/C_s$, $y\leftarrow y/C_s$.
\end{enumerate}
The penalty $\sigma$ lives in scaled units: on the original data it corresponds to $\sigma\, b_s/C_s$.
Termination uses the residuals of \cite[eqs.~(12)--(13)]{cuadmm} in original units,
\[
\eta_p = \frac{\norm{\A X - b}}{1+\norm{b}},\quad
\eta_d = \frac{\norm{\Ad y + S - C}}{1+\norm{C}},\quad
\eta_g = \frac{|\inner{C}{X}-\inner{b}{y}|}{1+|\inner{C}{X}|+|\inner{b}{y}|},\quad
\eta = \max(\eta_p,\eta_d,\eta_g),
\]
and, at termination and in validation mode, the cone violations of $X$ and $S$.

\subsection{The penalty parameter}\label{sec:penalty}

\paragraph{Rules in the code today.} All of them count ``wins'' after every iteration ($w_p$ if
$\eta_p<\eta_d$, else $w_d$) and act only at the check iterations
$k\in\{k\le200: k\equiv1\ (10)\}\cup\{200<k\le1000: k\equiv1\ (25)\}\cup\{1000<k\le5000: k\equiv1\ (50)\}
\cup\{5000<k\le10^4: k\equiv1\ (200)\}\cup\{k>10^4: k\equiv1\ (1000)\}$, where
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{if } w_p > \max(g,\ 1.35\,w_d): && \sigma\leftarrow\min(\sigma_{\max},\,f\sigma),\quad w_p\leftarrow0;\\
&\text{else if } w_d > \max(g,\ 1.35\,w_p): && \sigma\leftarrow\max(\sigma_{\min},\,\sigma/f),\quad w_d\leftarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $X$ is the multiplier of $\Ad y + S = C$, a larger $\sigma$ weights dual feasibility more. The
winner-count idiom (winner above $\max(1,\text{ratio}\cdot\text{other})$, winner-only reset) is the one used in
the SDPNAL+/SuiteLasso family of codes \cite{sdpnalplus}; the ratio, factor, schedule and run length here
differ from those codes \cite{sigmastudy}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{legacy_adaptive} at \code{9bb4ba5}: $g = 0$ (no guard), $f = 2$, $[\sigma_{\min},\sigma_{\max}] =
  [10^{-6},10^{6}]$, plus the ``chasing'' monitor (\code{Monitor1}) after $k = 5000$, which could move
  $\sigma$ to $\max(\sigma,100)$ or $\min(\sigma,0.01)$.
  \item The October 13 rule, committed in place in \code{2d8a755} under the same name: $g = 1$, $f = 2$,
  $[10^{-3},10^{3}]$, \code{Monitor1} off. Plan name: \code{residual_balancing}. An uncommitted edit of
  October 20 sets $f = 1.2$; it becomes the option \code{penalty.factor} (Pre-0).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why neither rule is enough.} Both keep checking every 1{,}000 iterations forever. On the GPU the
old rule changed $\sigma$ at 95.7\% of its checks; in the CPU replica the October 13 rule changed it at
70--100\% of the checks after $k = 10^4$ on nine small problems, and locked \code{trto1} and \code{buck1}
into exact periodic orbits that are not solutions, while fixed $\sigma = 1$ solves both \cite{sigmastudy}. The
varying-penalty convergence results that let $\sigma$ move both ways require $\sigma$ to become fixed after
finitely many iterations \cite{boyd2011} or its variation to be summable \cite{lorenz2019}; neither rule
provides that by construction. Removing \code{Monitor1} removed the $\times100$ to $\times16{,}384$ jumps that
preceded most large error spikes, but also the large-$\sigma$ excursions behind the old rule's speed on
\code{vibra1} \cite{sigmastudy}.

\paragraph{Planned rule: balance, then freeze.} Keep the residual balancing during the early, dense part
of the schedule---which is where the adaptive gains on the \code{mater} problems come from---and freeze
$\sigma$ at a well-chosen value once it stops making progress:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=plain]
policy balance_freeze                 (planned default; initial parameters in Table 2)
state: sigma, w_p, w_d, step = factor, dirs = [], sigmas = [], frozen = false

reset(sigma0): sigma = clamp(sigma0, sigma_min, sigma_max); w_p = w_d = 0; dirs = sigmas = []

update(k, eta_p, eta_d):              # residuals of the new iterate, original units
    if frozen: return sigma
    if residual_weight * eta_p < eta_d: w_p += 1 else: w_d += 1
    if is_check(k):                   # the 10/25/50/200/1000 schedule
        if   w_p > max(1, win_ratio * w_d): w_p = 0; change(k, +1)
        elif w_d > max(1, win_ratio * w_p): w_d = 0; change(k, -1)
    if k >= K_f:                                              freeze(k, "K_f")
    elif k >= k_min and len(dirs) >= W and |sum(dirs[-W:])| <= net_max:   freeze(k, "ping-pong")
    return sigma

change(k, d):                         # d = +1 raises sigma, d = -1 lowers it
    if step_schedule == halve_on_reversal and dirs and d == -dirs[-1]: step = sqrt(step)
    sigma = clamp(sigma * step^d, sigma_min, sigma_max); dirs.append(d); sigmas.append(sigma)
    if step_schedule == halve_on_reversal and log2(step) < 1/16: freeze(k, "step")

freeze(k, reason):
    if len(dirs) >= W and |sum(dirs[-W:])| <= net_max:        # sigma is bouncing in a band:
        sigma = geometric_mean(sigmas[-W:])                    # freeze at the centre of the band
    sigma = clamp(bias * sigma, sigma_min, sigma_max); frozen = true; log(k, reason, sigma)
\end{lstlisting}
The ping-pong test uses the \emph{net} movement over the last $W$ changes rather than a count of reversals:
the \code{trto1} orbit has the pattern $+\,+\,-\,-$, in which only half of the consecutive pairs reverse, so a
reversal count would miss it, while its net movement is zero. Steady drift ($+\,+\,+\,\dots$) has net movement $W$
and never triggers the freeze, so $\sigma$ can still travel to where it is needed.

\paragraph{What the freeze guarantees, and what it does not.} The rule changes $\sigma$ at most once per
check before the freeze, so finitely often; after the last change the iteration is fixed-$\sigma$ ADMM with
$\tau\in(0,2)$ started from the current point, whose iterates converge \cite{afterimage,chen2021}. The local
linear rate under strict complementarity \cite{kang2025local} is also a fixed-$\sigma$ result. Speed is not
guaranteed: in the replica, a freeze at $2\cdot10^4$ converged wherever any rule converged but was slower
than the best rule on four of the five problems it solved \cite{review20}, and the frozen value mattered
more than the freeze time \cite{sigmastudy}. Hence the band-centre value, the optional bias, and the
Phase-4b screen. The vote still ignores the duality gap $\eta_g$; a gap-aware vote is a research item
(Section~\ref{sec:decisions}).

\section{Assessment of the current code}\label{sec:assessment}

\subsection{What must be preserved}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The numerics: the iteration matches \eqref{eq:ystep}--\eqref{eq:xstep}; the three cones project
  onto the right dual cones; the $\svec$ conventions, the permutations of the CHOLMOD factor, unscaling and
  the residuals in original units are correct.
  \item The test suite (87 tests in the plain-ADMM build), which generates problems with a known strictly
  complementary primal--dual solution for PSD, free and nonnegative blocks and for every projection tier,
  and keeps golden $\sigma$ histories. On the reference workstation (2$\times$ RTX 6000 Ada, CUDA 12.9,
  SuiteSparse 7.6.1) 85 pass: the golden test fails by construction (B14) and the rank-deficient CHOLMOD test
  fails with this SuiteSparse version (B9; it passes with 7.10.1 on the reviewers' H200 \cite{review20}).
  \item Bitwise reproducibility of the exact paths (deterministic \code{CUSPARSE_SPMV_CSR_ALG2}), the
  external validator, the certified lower bound, the LOBPCG accept-or-fall-back logic, and the error checks
  on library calls (with the gaps listed as B15--B16).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Structural problems}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{P\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{One class does everything.} \code{SDPSolver} (\code{include/cuadmm/solver.h},
  \code{src/solver.cu}, 2{,}230 lines at \code{main}) has over 200 public data members; \code{init()} has 23
  parameters and about 590 lines, and \code{solve()} spans lines 1040--2119. \code{solve()} mixes the ADMM
  steps, the three PSD tiers with LOBPCG and composite filtering, the $\sigma$ rules, termination, external
  validation, best-iterate tracking, printing, and bookkeeping for a mode that is compiled out.
  \item \textbf{Projection logic at two levels.} Large blocks use member functions
  (\code{large_full_eig_project}, \code{large_lobpcg_project}, \code{large_composite_project}); medium and
  small blocks are inline in \code{solve()}. The buffers, handles and streams of all tiers are solver fields.
  \item \textbf{Configuration in four places.} The 23 parameters of \code{init()}, 10 of \code{solve()},
  public fields set before either call, and \texttt{\#define} constants (tier limits 32/1000, LOBPCG
  thresholds, composite margins). The CLI and MATLAB re-encode defaults independently, which produced B3
  and B4, and the C++ API has defaults of its own (Section~\ref{sec:api-defaults}).
  \item \textbf{Dead or compiled-out code.} \code{analyze_blk}, \code{get_maps}, \code{MatrixSizes::init}
  (all superseded by \code{BlockStructure}), \code{get_eig_rank_mask}, \code{max_dense_vector_zero_mask}
  and the \code{use_mask} path, \code{check_gpus}, \code{bscale2}/\code{Cscale2}, the commented-out monitor,
  and the sGS code that the CMake library compiles out but still threads through \code{solve()}.
  \item \textbf{Scaling in four places.} Applied in \code{init()}, re-applied for warm starts in
  \code{solve()}, undone at the end, and undone again on the host in \code{validate_current_iterate}.
  \item \textbf{Ad hoc streams.} The legacy default stream and non-blocking streams are mixed with manual
  synchronizations; B2 is a consequence.
  \item \textbf{Cone-specific branches in seven live files.} Conversion kernel, \code{solver.cu},
  external validator, block structure, problem loader, CLI, MATLAB binding.
  \item \textbf{Memory sized for concurrency that never happens.} The medium tier allocates one
  eigensolver workspace per matrix although at most \code{--streams} of them run at once (B20). The large
  tier keeps three dense $n_b\times n_b$ buffers and one EVD workspace per block although its blocks are
  projected one at a time (B19).
  \item \textbf{The PSD projection depends on a submodule} whose kernels, helper names, error macros and
  handle conventions differ from the solver's (B1, B10, B15).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Design principles}\label{sec:principles}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{Layers with one-way dependencies.} Front-ends $\to$ solver facade $\to$ algorithm $\to$
  components $\to$ core $\to$ CUDA libraries (Figure~\ref{fig:layers}). No component knows the solver.
  \item \textbf{Strategies, not branches.} Every algorithmic choice (iteration scheme, penalty policy, PSD
  projector, normal-equation backend, stopping rule) is an object chosen at runtime from options. No
  compile-time algorithm flags.
  \item \textbf{One source of truth for options.} Defaults live in \code{SolverOptions}; the CLI flags and
  MATLAB fields are generated from, or checked against, the same table.
  \item \textbf{Names never change meaning.} A behavior change gets a new option value or policy name; a
  removed or renamed name is an error, never a silent alias (B18).
  \item \textbf{Memory follows the access pattern.} Resources that are used concurrently are allocated per
  concurrent user; resources that are used one after another share one allocation sized for the largest
  user (Section~\ref{sec:psd}). Nothing is allocated inside the iteration loop.
  \item \textbf{Explicit GPU resource ownership.} Each component owns its streams, handles and workspace
  slices and declares cross-stream dependencies with events.
  \item \textbf{Math-to-code traceability.} Names follow Section~\ref{sec:math}: \code{y_step},
  \code{cone_step}, \code{multiplier_step}, \code{sigma}, \code{tau} (Appendix~\ref{app:notation}).
  \item \textbf{Deterministic by default.} Fixed seeds, deterministic SpMV, fixed reduction orders; faster
  nondeterministic modes are opt-in.
  \item \textbf{Behavior-preserving migration.} Structural phases reproduce baseline iterates bit for bit on
  each pinned machine; behavior changes are isolated in labelled phases with tolerance-based acceptance.
  \item \textbf{Testable components.} Each component can be driven from host data in a unit test without
  constructing a solver.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Algorithm layer}\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{The iteration loop}
After the reorganization the loop reads as the mathematics. Observers receive the residuals of the
iterate after the step, paired with the $\sigma$ that the next iteration will use, as the history does
today:
\begin{lstlisting}
SolveResult AdmmSolver::run(AdmmState& s) {
  KktResiduals r = kkt_.evaluate(s);                        // starting point, original units
  for (int k = 1; ; ++k) {
    if (const auto stop = stopping_.check(k - 1, r, s)) return finish(*stop, s);
    scheme_->iterate(s, sigma_, tau_);                      // y-step(s), cone step, multiplier step
    r = kkt_.evaluate(s);                                   // post-step residuals
    sigma_ = penalty_->update(k, r, sigma_);
    observers_.on_iteration(k, s, r, sigma_);
  }
}
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Termination, validation and the returned iterate}\label{sec:termination}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{KktEvaluator} computes $\eta_p,\eta_d,\eta_g$ once per iteration from the cached residual vectors
  and the \code{Scaling} object.
  \item Stopping rules implement \code{StoppingRule}: \code{InternalKktRule} (default), \code{ValidatedRule}
  (today's validation-aware stopping), \code{StrictDimacsRule}; all enforce the iteration and time limits
  and detect non-finite residuals.
  \item \code{ExternalValidator} stays a separate CPU implementation on the original data that uses none
  of the solver's GPU residuals or eigendecompositions. Each cone registers a host-side reference measure
  for it, kept apart from its GPU projection code.
  \item \code{Certificate} computes the cone terms of the certified lower bound through \code{Cone::measure}
  and needs an explicit bound per block where the cone requires one (free blocks: Section~\ref{sec:free}).
  \item \code{BestIterateTracker} ranks by the maximum DIMACS error when validations exist (B23), and
  \code{SolveResult} carries both the best and the final iterate.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reporting}
All bookkeeping that today lives in solver fields (\code{info_*} arrays, \code{sigma_log}, phase timers,
validation history) moves to \code{IterationObserver} implementations: console table, CSV history with 17
significant digits, $\sigma$ log, JSON summary, checkpoint writer. Observers never influence the iteration.

\subsection{Planned cones}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Second-order} $\mathcal{Q}^n = \{(t,x): \norm{x}\le t\}$ (code \code{q}), self-dual, closed form:
  $(t,x)$ if $\norm{x}\le t$; $0$ if $\norm{x}\le -t$; otherwise
  $\tfrac{t+\norm{x}}{2}\,(1, x/\norm{x})$. One batched kernel with a segmented reduction.
  \item \textbf{Rotated second-order} (code \code{r}) through the orthogonal map to $\mathcal{Q}^n$.
  \item \textbf{Exponential and power cones}: not self-dual, projections by a few Newton steps per cone;
  the interface separates primal and dual projections.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scaling compatibility}
The current scaling (row normalization of $\A$ and two scalars) is valid for every cone. A future variable
scaling must map each cone onto itself: any positive diagonal scaling for free and nonnegative blocks, a
congruence $X\mapsto DXD$ with positive diagonal $D$ for PSD blocks (entrywise $d_id_j$ in $\svec$
coordinates), and a uniform factor for second-order blocks. \code{scaling_compatibility()} makes this
checkable.

\section{Free variables}\label{sec:free}

Free variables are not a corner case: SOS formulations produce large free blocks (the SOS version of the
PushBox problem, \code{examples/SPOT/data/TXT/PushBox_N=30_SOS}, has one free block of 22{,}870 entries), and
modeling with equality-constrained auxiliary variables or SeDuMi's \code{K.f} produces them too. They are
handled correctly today; this section makes the handling explicit for every component, so that no part of
the reorganization can drop it.

\subsection{Mathematics}
For a free block $u$, $\K_u = \R^{n_u}$ and $\Kd_u = \{0\}$. In \eqref{eq:ystep}--\eqref{eq:xstep}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the y-step is unchanged: the columns of $\A$ that belong to $u$ are part of $\A\Ad$;
  \item the cone step sets $S_u = 0$;
  \item the multiplier step updates $X_u \leftarrow X_u + \tau\sigma(\A_u^* y - C_u)$, with no projection;
  \item at a solution, the dual constraint on the rows of $u$ is the equality $\A_u^* y = C_u$, and its
  residual is part of $\eta_d$; $X_u$ has no cone condition, so the primal solution set can be unbounded
  along $\ker\A_u$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Research option: enforce the free equalities in the y-step}
Instead of reaching $\A_u^* y = C_u$ only in the limit, the y-step can impose it exactly, as an affine
constraint on the $y$-block. With $r$ the non-free blocks, the y-step becomes the saddle-point system
\[
\begin{bmatrix} \A_r\A_r^* & \A_u \\ \A_u^* & 0 \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} y \\ \mu \end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} \sigma^{-1}b - \A_r(\sigma^{-1}X_r + S_r - C_r) \\ C_u \end{bmatrix},
\qquad X_u = \sigma\mu ,
\]
whose matrix does not depend on $\sigma$, so it is factorized once. It is still a two-block ADMM (the
$y$-block now carries the indicator of an affine set), but not iterate-by-iterate equivalent to
\eqref{eq:ystep}--\eqref{eq:xstep}. It needs an indefinite factorization: cuDSS in symmetric-indefinite mode,
or $LDL^\top$ of the regularized quasi-definite form with $-\delta I$ in the (2,2) block, which factors
without pivoting for any symmetric ordering \cite{vanderbei1995}. The \code{NormalEquationSolver} interface
reserves a saddle-point mode for it (Section~\ref{sec:linsys}); the option is evaluated on SOS problems
after Phase 7 and is not scheduled before.

\subsection{The large-block memory model}\label{sec:large-memory}
\paragraph{What has to persist.} Nothing of a large block's dense matrix: the cone-step input is rebuilt
every iteration from $\svec$ vectors, and the result is packed back into $S$. Only the per-block state
persists---the projection mode, the ranks and rank tolerance, and the LOBPCG warm start
$V_b\in\R^{n_b\times k_b^{\rm alloc}}$, $D_b\in\R^{k_b^{\rm alloc}}$ with
$k_b^{\rm alloc} = \lceil 1.2\rho\, n_b\rceil$. Everything else is transient and can be shared between blocks,
because they are processed one after another.

\paragraph{The arena.} At setup the large tier reserves one region of the \code{WorkspaceArena}, sized for
the largest large block $n_{\max}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:arena}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item $M$: $n_{\max}^2$ doubles, the dense input block; the EVD overwrites it with the eigenvectors;
  \item $W$: the largest workspace of any strategy at $n_{\max}$, queried from cuSOLVER at setup. On CUDA 12.9,
  \code{cusolverDnXsyevd} (FP64, vectors) needs $4.16\,n^2$ doubles at $n = 1000$ and $4.004\,n^2$ at
  $n = 30{,}000$ on the device, and no host workspace (measured). $W$ also hosts, at different times, the
  result $P$ of the final product ($n^2$), the LOBPCG buffers and the composite-filter buffers, which are all
  smaller than the EVD workspace;
  \item per block: $V_b$, $D_b$ and a few scalars.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Large-block pipeline and algorithms}\label{sec:large-alg}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=plain]
for each large block b, sequentially, on the large-tier stream:
    unpack  z_in(b) -> M                         # svec -> dense, O(n^2)
    pick    strategy from the large-block selection policy (below)
    project with M and W                         # result: S_b written to s_out(b)
    if rejected: unpack again (Z is not copied), then FullEvd with rank analysis
record event "large tier done"
\end{lstlisting}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=0.6cm]
  \item[FullEvd.] \code{cusolverDnXsyevd} in place: $M\leftarrow Q$, eigenvalues $\lambda_1\le\dots\le\lambda_n$.
  With $r_+$ positive and $r_-$ negative eigenvalues: if $r_+\le r_-$, scale the last $r_+$ columns of $Q$ in
  place by $\sqrt{\lambda_i}$ to get $B$, compute the lower triangle of $P = BB^\top$ with one SYRK into $W$, and
  pack it into $s_{\rm out}$; otherwise scale the first $r_-$ columns by $\sqrt{-\lambda_i}$, compute $P' =
  BB^\top$, and pack $S_b = Z_b + P'$, reading $Z_b$ from $z_{\rm in}$. The final product costs
  $n^2\min(r_+,r_-)\le n^3/2$ flops instead of $2n^3$ for today's GEMM, needs no $n^2$ temporary, and computes
  only the triangle that $\svec$ stores. When a rank analysis is due, the warm-start eigenvectors are copied
  to $V_b$ before the in-place scaling.
  \item[LOBPCG.] Works on $M = Z$; the negative mode passes $\alpha=-1$ to its products instead of negating $M$
  twice. Its buffers come from $W$ (B7). One product $Z[X\ R\ \Delta]$ ($n\times 3k$) per iteration serves both
  the residual and the Rayleigh--Ritz matrix, instead of the two products of today; the residual block is
  allocated $n\times k$, not $n\times 3k$. The result is formed by a SYRK on a scaled copy of the $k$ Ritz
  vectors (the warm start $V_b$ itself is not modified) and packed like FullEvd (adding $Z_b$ in the negative
  mode, without today's $n^2$ copy of $Z$).
  \item[Composite filter.] Lanczos estimate on $Z^2$, scaling, filter in the FP32/FP16 buffers of $W$, result in
  $M$, then the norm test and packing (Section~\ref{sec:composite}). On rejection the block is unpacked again
  and projected with FullEvd; today's $n^2$ copy of the input is no longer needed.
\end{description}
The memory model changes rounding in two places (per-block instead of strided-batched products for
equal-size large blocks, and SYRK instead of GEMM), so it is introduced in Phase 6b with tolerance-based
acceptance, after Phase 6a has ported the strategies bit for bit (option
\code{psd.large.final_product = gemm_compat} keeps the old product for comparisons).

\subsection{Small and medium tiers}\label{sec:batched}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Resident batches.} Each size group keeps its matrices in one batch buffer; the result buffer is
  separate. Today's third buffer ($Q\max(\Lambda,0)$) is dropped by scaling the columns of $Q$ in place by
  $\sqrt{\max(\lambda,0)}$ and forming $BB^\top$ with one strided-batched GEMM (Phase 6b: rounding changes).
  \item \textbf{Chunked batched EVD.} The batched workspace is $4.2$--$31\,n^2$ doubles per matrix (measured;
  the same as one \code{Xsyevd} per matrix), so groups are decomposed in chunks whose size follows from the
  memory budget (\code{psd.medium.max_batch}), instead of in one call per group.
  \item \textbf{Streamed EVD.} One workspace per stream, sized for the largest medium singleton, instead of
  one per matrix: 1{,}000 blocks of $n = 500$ need $15\times9.2$\,MiB $\approx137$\,MiB instead of $\approx8.9$\,GiB (B20).
  \item \textbf{Termination measures} (eigenvalues of $X$, $S$ and $C-\Ad y$) reuse the same machinery and
  workspaces; they do not keep per-matrix buffers alive for the whole solve.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Selection policy for large blocks}\label{sec:selection}
Per iteration and per large block, \code{LargeBlockPolicy} decides:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=plain]
warm(b)  = warmup != none and n_b >= warmup.min_size      # default: composite_fp16 and 5000
k_sw     = first k > 1 with eta < warmup.switch_tol or k > warmup.switch_iter   # global, one-way
start(b) = k_sw if warm(b) else lowrank.start_iter

if warm(b) and k < k_sw:
    CompositeFilter                      -> if rejected: FullEvd
elif lowrank.enabled and k >= start(b) and (k - start(b)) % lowrank.reevaluate == 0:
    FullEvd + RankAnalyzer               -> mode = LowRankPositive | LowRankNegative | Dense
elif mode(b) is LowRankPositive or LowRankNegative:
    Lobpcg(mode(b))                      -> if rejected: FullEvd + RankAnalyzer (same k)
else:
    FullEvd
\end{lstlisting}
Defaults (Q2, Q3): blocks with $n\ge5000$, the sizes evaluated in \cite{composite}, start with the FP16 filter;
large blocks with $1000<n<5000$ start with exact projections. The warm-up ends for all blocks at the first
iteration with $\eta<10^{-2}$ or $k>5000$, as today. The low-rank analysis then runs every 100 iterations,
from iteration 2 for blocks without a warm-up (today's CLI) and from the end of the warm-up for blocks with one
(today's C++ API), so no exact EVD of a warm-up block is paid during its warm-up; $\rho = 0.03$, relaxation
1.2. Today a single flag, \code{switched_proj_method}, sets both the warm-up and the low-rank start for every
block (B3).

\paragraph{Rank analysis.} With the eigenvalues of the block,
\[
\text{tol}_r = \max\big(10^{-8}\max(|\lambda_1|,|\lambda_n|),\ 10^{-3}\,\text{tol}\big),\qquad
r_+ = \#\{\lambda_i>\text{tol}_r\},\qquad r_- = \#\{\lambda_i<-\text{tol}_r\}.
\]
Positive mode is preferred when both ranks are below $\rho n$; the top (or bottom) $k$ eigenpairs are kept as
the warm start, with $k = \min(\lceil1.2\,r\rceil, k^{\rm alloc})$.

\paragraph{LOBPCG acceptance.} A result is accepted if LOBPCG converged, all Ritz values are finite, and the
$k$-th Ritz value is at most $\text{tol}_r$ plus the residual norm. This is necessary, not sufficient (Ritz
values are lower bounds); the periodic exact analysis and the cone violation reported at termination bound
the risk. The convergence tolerance becomes proportional to the stopping tolerance, with the constant
calibrated in Phase 6a (B13).

\subsection{Integrating the composite polynomial filter}\label{sec:composite}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Code move.} The FP16 composite kernel, the Lanczos norm estimate
  (\code{approximate_two_norm}), LOBPCG and the precision-conversion and symmetrization helpers move to
  \code{src/cone/psd/kernels/}, under cuADMM's error macros (B15) and with one copy of each helper (B10). The
  FP32 and FP32-emulated kernels (Q3), the WIP functions (\code{compute_eigenpairs},
  \code{*_auto_scale_deflate}) and the commented-out coefficient candidates stay in the frozen standalone
  repository; deflation is recorded as a possible future strategy.
  \item \textbf{Coefficients as data.} A \code{constexpr} table \code{kCompositeFp16[7][3]} holds the refined
  half-precision coefficients $\tilde f$ of \cite[Table~2]{composite}
  (identical to the code today), obtained by Remez initialization and gradient refinement, with a provenance
  comment; the Polar Express coefficients \cite{polarexpress} are kept only as a test baseline. Each step
  evaluates $aX + bX^3 + cX^5$ with three GEMMs ($X^2$, $X^3$, $X^3X^2$).
  \item \textbf{Algorithm per block.} Lanczos (20 steps) on $Z^2$ gives $\theta$ and $q$; the estimate
  $u = \sqrt{\theta + \norm{Z^2q - \theta q}}$ \cite[Theorem~2]{composite} is not a certified bound when the
  Ritz value is not closest to $\lambda_{\max}(Z^2)$, so it is scaled by a safety margin $\mu = 1.05$:
  $s = \mu u$, $X_0 = Z/s$, $X_t = \tilde f_t(X_{t-1}/d_t)$ for $t = 1,\dots,7$ with damping $d_t = 1.01$, result
  $P = s\cdot\tfrac12 X_0(I + X_T)$, symmetrized. The damping
  location (input, as coded, versus output, as described in \cite[\S3.4]{composite}) is documented (B11).
  \item \textbf{Precision and math modes (B1).} The GEMMs run on FP16 Tensor Cores with FP32 accumulation and
  output (\code{cublasGemmEx}, compute type \code{CUBLAS_COMPUTE_32F_FAST_16F} set in each call); the polynomial
  updates run in FP32 and the Lanczos estimate in FP64. The handle uses \code{CUBLAS_DEFAULT_MATH};
  \code{CUBLAS_TENSOR_OP_MATH} is never set. The FP16 output is bitwise identical under both modes (measured at
  $n = 1000$, 2000, 5000), so the B1 fix does not change it. Why FP16 only: the filter runs only while
  $\eta\ge10^{-2}$, where an error of about $10^{-3}$ does not limit progress; on H100/H200 (SXM) plain FP32 GEMMs peak at
  the FP64 Tensor Core rate (about 67 TFLOPS, against about 990 for dense FP16); and the FP32-emulated variant needs
  Blackwell GPUs that are not available for validation. A TF32 variant can be added later as a new option value.
  \item \textbf{Safety checks (B22, moved to Phase 1).} Reject and fall back to FullEvd if the result is not
  finite or if $\norm{P}_F > (1+10^{-2})\norm{Z}_F$: a free necessary condition, since the projection is
  nonexpansive with $\Proj(0)=0$. It also catches finite but wrong results from an underestimated scale.
  \item \textbf{Determinism (B8).} The Lanczos start vector comes from a counter-based generator seeded by
  \code{(options.seed, block index)}.
  \item \textbf{Workspace (B7).} The FP32/FP16 buffers and the Lanczos vectors live in $W$
  (Figure~\ref{fig:arena}); nothing is allocated per call.
  \item \textbf{Exposure (B4).} Options \code{psd.large.warmup} $\in$ \{\code{none}, \code{composite_fp16}\} and
  \code{psd.large.warmup.min_size}, in the CLI (\code{--psd-warmup}, \code{--psd-warmup-min-size}) and MATLAB.
  \item \textbf{Scope.} Large blocks with $n\ge$ \code{min_size}: the GEMM advantage needs $n$ in the thousands,
  and $n = 5000$ is the smallest size evaluated in \cite{composite}. Phase 6a benchmarks the filter against
  \code{FullEvd} at $n\in\{2000, 3000, 5000, 10000, 20000\}$ on the pinned GPUs to confirm or move the
  threshold. A strided-batched filter for medium groups is a benchmark item, not a default.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Linear systems}\label{sec:linsys}

\subsection{Role}
Every y-step solves $(\A\Ad + \varepsilon I)\,y = r$ with the scaled, row-normalized $\A$; ADMM needs one solve
per iteration and sGS-ADMM two. The matrix does not depend on $\sigma$, so a direct method factors it once
at setup, and the per-iteration \emph{solve} dominates the cost: SPOT runs are bound by CPU CHOLMOD solves of
5--93\,ms each \cite{review20}. Today CHOLMOD \cite{cholmod} always factors on the CPU (supernodal, falling
back to simplicial $LDL^\top$); solves run on the CPU with a device synchronization and two PCIe copies per
iteration, unless the factor is dense ($m\ge2000$, $m^2\le$ \code{INT_MAX}, $8m^2$ bytes $\le$ 25\% of free
memory, $\mathrm{nnz}(L) > 0.3\,m(m+1)/2$, simplicial $LDL^\top$ factor), in which case $L$ and $D$ are copied
to the GPU and each solve is two \code{cublasDtrsv} calls.

\subsection{Interface}
The interface is written for both exact and inexact solves, so that iterative methods can be added without
touching the algorithm layer:
\begin{lstlisting}
struct SolveRequest {
  double rel_tol = 0.0;           // 0: exact (direct backends); > 0: inexact solve allowed
  int max_iter = 0;               // iterative backends only
  bool use_initial_guess = false; // warm start from the content of y (e.g. the previous y-step)
};
struct SolveReport { int iterations = 0; double rel_residual = 0.0; bool converged = true; };

struct Capabilities { bool exact; bool device_solves; bool saddle_point; bool sigma_dependent_ok; };

class NormalEquationSolver {
 public:
  virtual ~NormalEquationSolver() = default;
  virtual std::string_view name() const = 0;
  virtual Capabilities capabilities() const = 0;
  virtual SymbolicInfo analyze(const NormalSystem& sys) = 0;    // ordering, nnz(L), memory
  virtual FactorInfo factorize(const NormalSystem& sys) = 0;    // no-op for matrix-free solvers
  virtual void update(const NormalSystem& sys) {}               // future sigma-dependent systems
  virtual SolveReport solve(DeviceSpan<const double> rhs, DeviceSpan<double> y,
                            const SolveRequest& req, ExecContext& ctx) = 0;
};

class NormalOperator {           // matrix-free: v -> (A A^T + eps I) v with two SpMVs (no A A^T formed)
 public:
  void apply(DeviceSpan<const double> v, DeviceSpan<double> out, ExecContext& ctx) const;
};
class Preconditioner {
 public:
  virtual ~Preconditioner() = default;
  virtual void setup(const NormalSystem& sys, ExecContext& ctx) = 0;
  virtual void apply(DeviceSpan<const double> r, DeviceSpan<double> z, ExecContext& ctx) const = 0;
};
\end{lstlisting}
\code{NormalSystem} describes $\A\Ad+\varepsilon I$ (assembled once, lower triangle, with \code{cusparseSpGEMM}
on the GPU or \code{cholmod_aat} on the host), optionally the saddle-point form of
Section~\ref{sec:free}, and the matrix-free operator. Today's system does not depend on $\sigma$;
\code{update()} exists for future variants whose system does (proximal terms, quadratic objectives), where a
penalty rule with finitely many changes also bounds the number of refactorizations.

\subsection{Backends}
\begin{description}
  \item[\code{CholmodSolver} (always built).] Today's behavior moved out of \code{SDPSolver}: supernodal with
  simplicial $LDL^\top$ fallback; CPU solves through pinned host buffers and asynchronous copies on the solver
  stream; the dense mode keeps all of today's conditions, so the CHOLMOD path stays bitwise.
  \item[\code{CudssSolver} (optional, \code{CUADMM_WITH_CUDSS}).] NVIDIA cuDSS \cite{cudss}, distributed
  separately from the CUDA toolkit and NVIDIA's replacement for the sparse Cholesky routines of cuSOLVER,
  which the CUDA 12.9 headers mark deprecated in favor of cuDSS. Analysis, factorization and solve on the
  device; SPD (Cholesky) or symmetric indefinite ($LDL^\top$) for rank-deficient and saddle-point systems; no
  host synchronization per solve. The minimum version is pinned, availability is checked at runtime, and its
  deterministic mode is enabled under \code{determinism = strict} (it is off by default in cuDSS).
  \item[\code{DenseCusolverSolver} (optional).] \code{cusolverDnXpotrf}/\code{Xpotrs} for SPD, \code{Dsytrf}/\code{Xsytrs}
  otherwise, or an explicit inverse factor from \code{Xtrtri} so each solve is two triangular matrix--vector
  products, which parallelize better than \code{trsv}. Useful when $m$ is a few thousand and the factor is
  dense. The reference GPUs have weak FP64 (about 1/64 of FP32), which penalizes dense FP64 factorizations.
\end{description}

\subsection{Selection}\label{sec:linsys-selection}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If \code{linsys.backend} is not \code{auto}, use it.
  \item Otherwise run the symbolic analysis (\code{cholmod_analyze}, or the cuDSS analysis phase once cuDSS is
  admitted to \code{auto}) to get $\mathrm{nnz}(L)$ and the memory need.
  \item Until the benchmark of Section~\ref{sec:bench} reports, \code{auto} chooses CHOLMOD with today's rule
  for the dense mode ($m\ge2000$, $m^2\le$ \code{INT_MAX}, $8m^2$ bytes within the budget,
  $\mathrm{nnz}(L)\ge0.3\,m(m+1)/2$, simplicial factor). After the benchmark, \code{auto} prefers cuDSS when its
  factor fits the budget.
  \item With \code{linsys.autotune = true}, factor with the two best candidates, time ten solves each and keep
  the faster. The choice and the timings go to the log and the summary.
\end{enumerate}
Throughout the bitwise phases the backend is CHOLMOD and presolve is off.

\subsection{Inexact and preconditioned solves (reserved)}\label{sec:pcg}
Direct factorizations stop scaling when the factor does not fit or its fill makes each solve expensive. The
interface above lets an iterative backend take over without changes elsewhere:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Solver.} Preconditioned CG on the matrix-free \code{NormalOperator}, warm-started from the
  previous $y$ (successive y-steps change little near convergence), with $O(m)$ vectors of memory.
  \item \textbf{Accuracy schedule.} Inexact y-steps are admissible when the errors are summable, as in the
  inexact sGS-ADMM of \cite{chen2017}: $\norm{(\A\Ad+\varepsilon I)\hat y - r}\le\epsilon_k$ with
  $\sum_k\epsilon_k<\infty$, for example $\epsilon_k = \min(\kappa\,\eta_{k-1},\ \epsilon_0 k^{-1.1})$. The
  policy that produces $\epsilon_k$ is a small strategy object of its own; \code{SolveReport} feeds the
  iteration count and residual to the observers.
  \item \textbf{Preconditioners}, behind the \code{Preconditioner} interface: Jacobi (weak here, since the row
  normalization makes the diagonal of $\A\Ad$ close to 1 for rows with $\norm{A_i}\ge1$); block-Jacobi over
  groups of constraints; incomplete Cholesky (in-house or third party: cuSPARSE's \code{csric02} is marked
  deprecated in CUDA 12.9); the exact factor of a nearby matrix, e.g.\ from the previous problem in a
  sequence of related trajectory-optimization solves; and mixed precision (an FP32 factor with FP64
  iterative refinement).
  \item \textbf{Scope.} Inexact solves change the iterates, so they are never used in the bitwise phases.
  The interface and \code{NormalOperator} land in Phase 7; \code{PcgSolver} and the preconditioners are a
  research item with their own benchmark.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Rank deficiency and presolve}
Moment relaxations often have dependent constraints (the SPOT data); a tiny regularization
$\varepsilon = 10^{-15}$ plus simplicial $LDL^\top$ makes them work today, and it is fragile (B9). A
\code{Presolve} step detects dependent rows from the factorization pivots (or a sparse QR), removes them after
checking that $b$ is consistent, and maps $y$ back at the end. Regularization stays as a fallback option.
Presolve changes $b$, hence $b_s$ and the meaning of a given $\sigma$, so it is off during the bitwise phases
and its effect is measured in Phase 7. cuDSS in SPD mode also needs a nonsingular matrix.

\subsection{Benchmark protocol}\label{sec:bench}
Run early, before cuDSS can become a default. Problems: SPOT (PushBox, PushT, PlanarHand at $N=1,10,30$,
including the SOS form), pendulum (\code{licols}), and PLATO / Mittelmann instances including one with a
dense factor (\code{neosfrbr25}). Metrics: analysis, factorization and per-solve time; peak memory; relative
residual $\norm{\A\Ad y - r}/\norm{r}$; end-to-end time to $\eta<10^{-4}$. GPUs: the RTX 6000 Ada workstation
and an H100/H200 node.

\section{Problem input and output}\label{sec:io}

\paragraph{Today.} The solver reads one format: a directory of text files (\code{blk.txt}; \code{At.txt} with
0-based \code{row col value} triplets of $\Ad$ in $\svec$ form; \code{b.txt}; \code{C.txt}; \code{con_num.txt}). Every
other format is converted outside the solver, by MATLAB scripts (\code{examples/sdpt3_to_txt.m},
\code{sedumi_to_txt.m}, \code{mosek_to_txt.m}) or by the Python SDPA converter of the PLATO experiments; the MEX
takes the same arrays in memory. The text reader parses about $3.5\times10^6$ nonzeros per second: 20 million
nonzeros take 708\,MB of text and 5.7\,s, against 320\,MB and 0.16\,s for the same data in binary read from the
page cache (measured on the reference workstation). It also builds COO triplets before CSC, doubling the host
memory, and stores sizes in \code{int} (B27). At $10^9$ nonzeros that would be about 35\,GB of text and five
minutes of parsing at every run.

\paragraph{Design.} Every reader produces the same \code{ProblemData}; all conventions (objective sense, $\svec$
layout, block mapping) live in \code{src/io/} and nowhere else. Sizes are 64-bit in the readers and in the native
format; setup checks that the problem fits the solver's current 32-bit indexing and says so if it does not.
Readers fill the CSC arrays directly, without a COO copy. Each reader prints a conversion report (what was
mapped, mirrored, merged or simplified), which also goes into the summary.

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=0.6cm]
\item[SDPA.] SDPA's dual (D) is cuADMM's primal: $C = -F_0$, $A_i = F_i$, $b_i = c_i$, and the returned $y$ is
$-x_{\rm SDPA}$. Negative block sizes are diagonal blocks (\code{l}). Lower-triangle entries are mirrored;
duplicate entries, non-finite values and indices out of range reject the file; explicit zeros are dropped; the
DIMACS block kinds are kept for the error measures.
\item[SeDuMi.] \code{K.f} $\to$ \code{u}, \code{K.l} $\to$ \code{l}, \code{K.s} $\to$ \code{s}; \code{K.q} and \code{K.r} with
Phase 9; complex data (\code{K.scomplex}, \code{K.xcomplex}) is rejected. \code{A} ($m\times N$ or $N\times m$) and
\code{At} ($N\times m$) are both accepted; when $N = m$ the name decides the orientation. PSD blocks are full
column-major $n^2$ vectors and need not be symmetric: the $\svec$ entry of $(k,l)$, $k<l$, is $(A_{kl}+A_{lk})/\sqrt2$.
PLATO's \code{.mat} files are in this format (with \code{At}).
\item[SDPT3.] \code{blk{p,1}} $\in$ \{\code{s}, \code{l}, \code{u}\} (\code{q} with Phase 9). An \code{s} entry that lists several
sizes is split into separate blocks. \code{At{p}} is already in $\svec$ form with the same convention as cuADMM;
\code{C{p}} is converted.
\item[MOSEK \code{prob}.] Bar variables (\code{bardim}) become \code{s} blocks. \code{bara} and \code{barc} list the lower
triangle ($\text{subk}\ge\text{subl}$) of symmetric matrices, so the $\svec$ value is \code{val} on the diagonal and
$\sqrt2\,\text{val}$ off it. Scalar variables (columns of \code{a}): missing or infinite bounds $\to$ \code{u},
$[0,\infty)$ $\to$ \code{l}, other bounds by a shift and, for a finite upper bound, a slack. Rows with
\code{blc} $=$ \code{buc} are equalities; other rows get a slack in an \code{l} block. \code{cfix} is the objective
offset. \code{cones} and \code{accs}/\code{f}/\code{g} come with Phase 9; integer variables are rejected. The struct does
not store the objective sense, which is an argument of \code{mosekopt}: the reader takes it from a variable
\code{sense} saved next to \code{prob}, or from \code{io.sense}, and stops if neither is given, because SPOT's SOS files
are maximization problems and a silent default would solve a different problem.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Exact simplifications at read time} (option \code{io.simplify}, on by default, each one reported).
A $1\times1$ PSD block becomes an \code{l} block. A PSD block whose data ($\A$ and $C$) touch only its diagonal
becomes an \code{l} block of the same size; this is exact, because such an $X\succeq0$ can be replaced by
$\operatorname{diag}(X)\ge0$ without changing the optimal value, and the dual slack is diagonal. It matters for
PLATO's SeDuMi files: \code{buck3.mat} stores the 544-entry diagonal block of \code{buck3.dat-s} as a
$544\times544$ PSD block, which would cost an $n = 544$ eigendecomposition per iteration instead of 544 scalar
operations; read with this simplification, \code{buck3.mat} gives the same data as \code{buck3.dat-s} to within
rounding (checked). Solutions are mapped back to the file's block structure on output. The legacy TXT reader
reads files exactly as today and never simplifies, so results on existing TXT data do not change.

\paragraph{Output.} Solutions are written in the convention of the input: \code{.h5} (group \code{/solution});
SDPA ($x = -y$, $X$, $Y$); SeDuMi (\code{x}, \code{y}, \code{z} as full vectors); SDPT3 (cell arrays); MOSEK (the
\code{res.sol.itr} fields \code{barx} and \code{bars} as lower triangles, \code{xx}, \code{slx}, \code{y}), so that SPOT's
\code{recover_mosek_sol_blk} works unchanged. Objective values are reported in the file's sense, including the
offset.

\paragraph{Native format.} One HDF5 file, version 1:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=plain]
/                  attrs: format = "cuadmm", version = 1, sense = "min" | "max",
                          objective_offset, source, source_sha256,
                          svec = "upper, column-major, off-diagonal x sqrt(2)"
/blocks/kind       uint8 (B)       's', 'l', 'u' ('q' later)
/blocks/size       int64 (B)
/At/colptr         int64 (m+1)     CSC of A^T: one column per constraint
/At/rowind         int64 (nnz)     svec index, 0-based, sorted within each column
/At/val            float64 (nnz)
/b/index, /b/val   int64, float64  sparse
/C/index, /C/val   int64, float64  sparse
/meta/...          optional: DIMACS block kinds, conversion report
/start/...         optional warm start: X, y, S, sigma
/solution/...      written by the solver: X, y, S, status, residuals, objective values
\end{lstlisting}
Datasets are chunked (about $10^6$ entries per chunk) and optionally compressed; the reader copies chunks
straight into the CSC arrays. MATLAB writes the file with \code{h5create}/\code{h5write} and Python with
\code{h5py}; the tool \code{cuadmm-convert} converts any supported format into it once, for problems too large to
parse at every run.

\paragraph{Dependencies and detection.} HDF5 and matio (BSD-style licenses), and zlib for \code{.gz}. Build options
\code{CUADMM_WITH_HDF5} and \code{CUADMM_WITH_MATIO} are on when the libraries are found; without matio, the
\code{.mat} formats are read by the MATLAB and Python front-ends, which pass the variables in memory. The CLI lists
the formats compiled in. The format is detected from the extension and, for \code{.mat}, from the variables
present (\code{prob} $\to$ MOSEK; \code{blk} with \code{At}, \code{C}, \code{b} $\to$ SDPT3; \code{K} with \code{A} or
\code{At} $\to$ SeDuMi), and can be forced with \code{io.format}.

\paragraph{SPOT.} SPOT builds the MOSEK \code{prob} struct from coordinate lists produced by its C++ core
(\code{mosek_standard_sdp_test_1.m} and \code{_2.m}). Moment relaxations are minimizations over bar variables with
\code{barc}; SOS relaxations are maximizations with free scalar variables and no \code{barc}. The sample files in
\code{examples/SPOT/data/MOSEK} are MATLAB v5 files that contain only \code{prob}; read with the mapping above, three
of them (PushBox $N=30$ moment and SOS, PushT $N=10$ moment) reproduce their TXT conversions in
\code{examples/SPOT/data/TXT} to within rounding (checked). Two changes on the SPOT side make
the hand-over exact and fast (O9): save \code{sense} next to \code{prob}, and, for large instances, write the cuADMM
HDF5 file directly from the same coordinate lists.

\section{Scaling}\label{sec:scaling}
A \code{Scaling} object holds $\nu$, $b_s$, $C_s$ and provides \code{scale_problem}, \code{scale_iterate},
\code{unscale_iterate}, \code{to_original_residuals} and \code{sigma_to_original}. It is the only code that
knows the scaling; the warm start, the termination, the validator and the result use it. Option
\code{scaling.enabled} (default true) allows running unscaled for diagnostics. Two known limitations are
recorded, not changed: the residual vote is not invariant under rescaling $b$ and $C$, because of the ``$1+$''
in its normalizers \cite{wohlberg2017,sigmastudy}, and $\sigma_0$ is given in scaled units. The unused fields
\code{bscale2}, \code{Cscale2} are deleted.

\section{Core infrastructure}\label{sec:core}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\code{GpuContext}}: device, library handles per stream role, cuDSS handle, and the math mode of
  each handle set explicitly at creation (B1).
  \item \textbf{\code{StreamPool} and \code{ExecContext}}: named streams (main, small, medium[$N$], large,
  linsys, copy). An \code{ExecContext} bundles a stream, its handles and a workspace slice; cross-stream
  dependencies use events. Everything runs on non-blocking streams owned by the pool, which removes the class
  of bugs behind B2. A \code{single_stream} mode runs everything on one stream; it must reproduce the
  concurrent run bit for bit, because \code{compute-sanitizer racecheck} cannot see B2-type races between
  streams in global memory.
  \item \textbf{\code{WorkspaceArena}}: components declare \code{WorkspaceRequest}s at setup; the arena
  allocates once, hands out aligned slices, and lets components that never run at the same time share a
  region (the large tier, Section~\ref{sec:large-memory}). No \code{cudaMalloc}/\code{cudaFree} inside the loop.
  \item \textbf{Buffers}: \code{DeviceBuffer<T>}, \code{PinnedHostBuffer<T>}, \code{DeviceSpan<T>} with
  \code{std::size_t} sizes; the overflow checks of \code{BlockStructure} are kept.
  \item \textbf{Checks}: one set of error macros (B15), a launch check after every kernel (B16), exceptions
  instead of \code{exit()}.
  \item \textbf{Determinism}: option \code{determinism} $\in$ \{\code{strict} (default), \code{fast}\}.
  \item \textbf{Logging}: a \code{Logger} with levels; library code does not call \code{printf}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Testing, benchmarking and continuous integration}\label{sec:testing}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Unit tests} per component: cone properties (idempotence, Moreau decomposition, orthogonality,
  nonexpansiveness, membership) for every cone, the free cone included; every PSD projector against an FP64
  reference on synthetic spectra (full rank, low rank $\pm$, clustered, tiny $n$, $n=1000$, $n>1000$), with
  tolerances per precision (FP16 composite below $2\times10^{-3}$ median relative error, exact projectors near
  machine precision); SYRK versus GEMM products on both eigen-sides; normal-equation
  backends on full-rank and rank-deficient matrices; penalty policies on scripted residual sequences at full
  precision; option parsing, validation and unknown names.
  \item \textbf{Memory tests}: the setup-time memory plan against the measured peak, for one and several
  large blocks, and a block at the EVD limit on a large-memory GPU.
  \item \textbf{Integration tests}: generated problems with known optimum for every cone (free blocks as in
  Section~\ref{sec:free}) and every projector path, both schemes, $\tau\in\{1.618,1.918\}$, both direct backends.
  \item \textbf{Golden histories}: per-iteration $(\text{pobj},\text{dobj},\eta_p,\eta_d,\sigma)$ of a fixed set
  of problems, per policy, regenerated only by the published generator script when a change is intended.
  \item \textbf{Formats}: each reader on hand-made edge cases (lower-triangle and duplicate entries, block
  groups, bounds and ranges in MOSEK data, missing sense, $N = m$ in SeDuMi); instances available in several
  formats (PLATO SDPA and SeDuMi, SPOT MOSEK and TXT) give the same \code{ProblemData} up to the reported
  simplifications; HDF5 round trips; solution output read back by SPOT's \code{recover_mosek_sol_blk}.
  \item \textbf{End to end}: a small SPOT (including SOS) and PLATO subset checked against MOSEK.
  \item \textbf{Benchmarks} (\code{bench/}): projector micro-benchmarks, normal-equation backends, end-to-end
  time; per-iteration time must stay within about 2\% on pendulum $N = 80$ and three PLATO problems
  (run-to-run noise is about 0.6\% \cite{review20}).
  \item \textbf{CI}: build matrix with and without cuDSS; CUDA 12.8 and 12.9; explicit CUDA architectures
  (today \code{CUDA_ARCHITECTURES OFF}); nightly \code{compute-sanitizer} (memcheck, racecheck, synccheck) and the
  single-stream bitwise check; GPU jobs through Slurm scripts until CI has GPU runners.
\end{itemize}

\section{Migration plan}\label{sec:migration}
Each step is one or a few pull requests on a feature branch, merged in order through pull requests (the
\code{main} ruleset requires them). ``Bitwise'' means identical per-iteration histories on the golden set and
on two real problems, on each pinned machine and configuration (GPU model and SM count, driver, CUDA,
SuiteSparse and BLAS versions, thread count, CUDA architectures).

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=0.6cm]
\item[Pre-0 --- Names and housekeeping.] In today's \code{SDPSolver}: restore \code{legacy_adaptive} to
\code{9bb4ba5} bit for bit (no guard, $[10^{-6},10^{6}]$, \code{Monitor1}); add \code{residual_balancing} with the
October 13 rule and the options \code{penalty.factor} (default 2; the working-tree 1.2 becomes an option
value); make \code{residual_balancing} the default until Phase 4b (O1); write the policy, its parameters and the
build commit into the summary JSON; make unknown policy names an error; fix the CLI help and README (B18).
Housekeeping: drop the LaTeX \code{.synctex.gz} from the tree; the experiment authors publish the golden
generator, push and tag the cited commits, and fix the dangling references (B26).
\emph{Acceptance:} the legacy golden test passes again (B14); \code{residual_balancing} goldens are generated
by the published script.
\item[Phase 0 --- Safety net.] Fixed-$\sigma$ baselines first: they do not depend on the penalty policy, and
\code{main} must reproduce the PLATO fixed-$\sigma$ histories bit for bit (\code{trto1}, \code{mater-1}; under
0.1 H200-hour). One baseline per pinned machine and configuration; GPU gate scripts; the CHOLMOD rank-deficient
test made independent of the SuiteSparse version (B9). \emph{Acceptance:} baselines stored; CI green.
\item[Phase 1 --- Cleanup and one-line fixes.] Delete dead code (P4); remove \code{CUADMM_PLAIN_ADMM_ONLY} (B5);

\item[Phase 2 --- Options and front-ends.] \code{SolverOptions}, the \code{Solver} facade over the existing

\item[Phase 3 --- Thin core.] \code{GpuContext}, \code{ExecContext}, buffer and span types, unified checks (B15).

\item[Phase 4 --- Algorithm layer.] \code{AdmmState}, \code{IterationScheme}, the penalty policies of

\item[Phase 4b --- Tune and adopt \code{balance_freeze}.] The default policy must have finite total $\sigma$

gates (at most 12.6 H200-hours): G0 fixed-$\sigma$ reproduction; G1 \code{trto1}, \code{buck1} (600\,s caps),

$\sum|\log_2\Delta\sigma|$ stays bounded and the changes stop; no change followed by a $\ge10\times$ rise in the
validated error; a better shifted geometric mean of time (shift 10\,s) than both \code{residual_balancing} and
fixed $\sigma = 1$; robust across $\sigma_0$. \emph{Outcome:} the best passing setting becomes the default
of \code{penalty.policy}. If no setting passes all gates, adopt the one that loses the fewest problems,
document the losses, and revisit the freeze design (O2, O8).

\item[Phase 6b --- PSD memory model.] Stream pool and arena; large-block arena and per-block pipeline

\item[Phase 7 --- Linear systems.] The interface types (\code{NormalSystem}, \code{NormalOperator}, and

\item[Phase 9 --- Second-order cones (deferred until needed).] As in Section~\ref{sec:cones}, with generator

\item[Phase 10 --- Documentation and release.] README rewrite, Doxygen, examples on the new options, migration

\paragraph{Milestones and effort.} Milestone 1 is Pre-0 to 4b, including 2b; Phases 5 to 10 are re-planned once
it lands. Milestone 2 is Phases 5--6b (cones, free variables, PSD), milestone 3 is Phase 7. The reorganization
touches about 9{,}200 library and MEX lines, 2{,}900 submodule lines and 6{,}700 test lines \cite{review20}; the
new readers and writers of Phase 2b add an estimated 3{,}000--4{,}000 lines including tests. The solver
stays single-GPU. Neither repository has a license yet, and CHOLMOD includes GPL-licensed modules (O4).

\begin{warnbox}{Risks and mitigations}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{Silent behavior changes} --- bitwise acceptance on each pinned machine for the structural phases;
  every intended change gets its own pull request, option value and golden files.
  \item \emph{The default penalty policy loses problems} --- Phase 4b gates against both the old rule and fixed
  $\sigma$; \code{legacy_adaptive} and \code{fixed} stay selectable.
  \item \emph{Memory regressions} --- the setup-time memory plan is tested against measured peaks.
  \item \emph{cuDSS availability and version drift} --- optional dependency with a pinned version; CHOLMOD
  remains the guaranteed backend.
  \item \emph{Performance regressions from abstraction} --- virtual calls happen once per component per
  iteration, not per element; the 2\% per-iteration threshold runs in CI.
  \item \emph{Reproducing published results} --- \code{legacy_adaptive} and tagged, published commits.
\end{itemize}
\end{warnbox}

\section{Decisions}\label{sec:decisions}

\subsection{Adopted from the review}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Q\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{Penalty history.} \code{legacy_adaptive} = \code{9bb4ba5}, kept through the migration as the
  bitwise baseline and Phase-4b comparator; the October 13 rule is \code{residual_balancing}; the default must
  have finite $\sigma$ variation: \code{balance_freeze}, tuned and adopted in Phase 4b.
  \item \textbf{Low-rank start.} Per block: from iteration 2 for blocks without a warm-up, as in today's CLI;
  from the end of the warm-up for blocks with one, as in today's C++ API.
  \item \textbf{Warm-up.} FP16 only: \code{composite_fp16} by default for large blocks with $n\ge5000$, none
  below; switched on in Phase 6a. The FP32 and FP32-emulated filters are not ported. This replaces the review's
  recommendation (\code{none} until Phase 6 shows a gain); the Phase-6a benchmark confirms or moves the
  threshold.
  \item \textbf{$\tau$.} Keep 1.618. Sweep $\{1.618, 1.9, 1.95\}$ at fixed $\sigma$, then re-screen the penalty at
  the chosen $\tau$; avoid $\tau\ge1.99$ from cold starts. 1.918 stays available.
  \item \textbf{Hybrid mode.} Decide after plain ADMM with $\tau=1.95$; probably retired.
  \item \textbf{MATLAB.} \code{cuadmm.solve(At, b, C, K, opts)} with a positional shim for one release.
  \item \textbf{cuDSS.} Optional, pinned minimum version, runtime check, deterministic mode under \code{strict};
  admitted to \code{auto} after the benchmark.
  \item \textbf{Python.} After Phases 2 and 4; public types without raw pointers.
  \item \textbf{\code{psd_projection}.} Tagged and frozen; the submodule is removed after Phase 6a's parity tests.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Still open}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{O\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{Default policy before Phase 4b}: \code{residual_balancing} (the behavior of \code{main}
  since \code{2d8a755}, proposed) or \code{legacy_adaptive} (the goldens and published runs).
  \item \textbf{Parameters of \code{balance_freeze}}: decided by the Phase-4b screen
  (Table~\ref{tab:freeze-params}), including the factor (2 or 1.2) and the step schedule.
  \item \textbf{Citation for $\tau\in(0,2)$.} \cite{afterimage} cites Gabay--Mercier for two-block ADMM ``when one
  objective block is linear''; the review reads Gabay--Mercier as assuming strong convexity of one block. This
  plan relies only on \cite{afterimage} and \cite{chen2021}; whether the citation in \cite{afterimage} needs
  adjusting is for its authors to decide.
  \item \textbf{License} for cuADMM, compatible with CHOLMOD's modules.
  \item \textbf{PSD blocks above the EVD limit} ($n>32{,}768$ on CUDA 12.9): reject (Phase 1), or later a CPU
  LAPACK fallback for the periodic exact projections. Multi-GPU eigensolvers are out of scope.
  \item \textbf{Exact free equalities in the y-step} (Section~\ref{sec:free}): evaluate on SOS problems after
  Phase 7.
  \item \textbf{Preconditioned iterative solves} (Section~\ref{sec:pcg}): priority and first preconditioner.
  \item \textbf{Gap-aware vote}: comparing weighted $\eta_p$ and $\eta_d$ so that the duality gap is balanced too
  \cite{sigmastudy}; untested.
  \item \textbf{SPOT hand-over} (Section~\ref{sec:io}): SPOT saves \code{sense} next to \code{prob}, and writes the
  cuADMM HDF5 file directly for large instances; for the SPOT maintainers.
\end{enumerate}
